## Key Question 1: What exact fee split does each platform use today, and how did it change over time?

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 pump.fun charges 1.25% on the bonding curve (0.30% creator / 0.95% protocol) and a market-cap-tiered 0.30%–1.25% on PumpSwap (creator share decays from 0.95% to 0.05%), with 50% of net fees hard-coded to PUMP buy-and-burn since 28 Apr 2026; Bonk.fun charges 1.5% on-curve (1.25% platform + 0.25% Raydium LaunchLab) with 51% of platform revenue now buying BONK for Nasdaq-listed Bonk, Inc.'s treasury (burn removed 3 Dec 2025); Bags gives creators a flat 1% of every trade, splittable among up to 100 recipients; Believe used ~2% (50/50 then 70/30 creator) before collapsing; Moonshot Create charges 0.5% pre-bond / 0.3% post-bond with up to half to creator; Boop charged 1% (60% stakers / 30% buyback / 10% team) and is now effectively dead; StonkFun lets creators pick a 1% or 2% pool fee and routes ~60% of platform revenue to STONK buy-and-burn.

### Cited Findings

#### pump.fun / PumpSwap: fee timeline
- Pump.fun launched 19 Jan 2024; by 2026 it had created ~12M tokens and passed $1B cumulative revenue, the first Solana app to do so — [CoinLaw, citing The Block](https://coinlaw.io/memecoin-statistics/)
- Before 20 Mar 2025, graduating tokens migrated to a Raydium pool and pump.fun deducted a 6 SOL migration fee; since PumpSwap launched the migration fee is 0.015 SOL deducted from liquidity — [soltokencreator graduation explainer (per search snippet)](https://www.soltokencreator.io/blog/pump-fun-graduation-explained); 0.015 SOL graduation fee also in [Coin Bureau, updated 1 Jul 2026](https://coinbureau.com/analysis/best-memecoin-launchpads)
- Creator revenue share ("Creator Fees") introduced for tokens created from 13 May 2025: creators earned 0.05% of each transaction, equal to 50% of protocol usage fees — [Smithii, Project Ascend article (pub. 3 Sep 2025, mod. 20 Jun 2026)](https://smithii.io/en/project-ascend-update/). Official docs date the mainnet Pump + PumpSwap upgrade adding the creator vault to "Monday, May 12, 11:00 AM UTC" — [pump-public-docs PUMP_CREATOR_FEE_README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/PUMP_CREATOR_FEE_README.md)
- Creators earned $2M in the first day under the May 2025 creator-fee structure — [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/pumpfun-creators-earn-dollar2m-in-first-day-under-new-fee-structure)
- Project Ascend / "Dynamic Fees V1" (liquidity fees effective 2 Sep 2025): creator share on all bonding-curve tokens raised 6x from 0.05% to 0.30%; on graduated tokens creator share ranges 0.05%–0.95% by market cap. Full PumpSwap tier table (market cap in SOL → creator / PumpSwap protocol / LP / total): <420 → 0.3% / 0.93% / 0.02% / 1.25%; 420–1,470 → 0.95% / 0.05% / 0.2% / 1.2%; 1,470–2,460 → 0.9% / 0.05% / 0.2% / 1.15%; then creator share steps down 0.05 pts per tier (2,460–3,440: 0.85%; 3,440–4,420: 0.80%; 4,420–9,820: 0.75%; 9,820–14,740: 0.70%; 14,740–19,650: 0.65%; 19,650–24,560: 0.60%; 24,560–29,470: 0.55%; 29,470–34,380: 0.50%; 34,380–39,300: 0.45%; 39,300–44,210: 0.40%; 44,210–49,120: 0.35%; 49,120–54,030: 0.30%), then 0.025 pts per tier (54,030–58,940: 0.275% … 93,330–98,240: 0.075%), floor >98,240 SOL → 0.05% creator / 0.05% protocol / 0.2% LP / 0.30% total. Pools created directly on PumpSwap (not via graduation) pay 0.25% LP fee and earn no creator rewards. Pump.fun's stated rationale: creator fees "proved the concept" but startup costs (viral marketing, exchange listings) need real money; raising fees on everyone would hurt traders, so dynamic fees apply only to graduated tokens — [Smithii](https://smithii.io/en/project-ascend-update/)
- Blockworks reported pump.fun adopting the dynamic fee model as its share surged past Bonk (page 403'd; per search snippet: fees scaled from 0.95% for tokens under ~$300K market cap down to 0.05% beyond ~$20M) — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/pumpdotfun-fee-model)
- Current (Oct 2026) numbers: bonding-curve trades 1.25% (0.30% creator, 0.95% platform; a $1,000 buy pays $12.50); PumpSwap 1.25% below 420 SOL mcap falling to 0.30% at ≥98,240 SOL; creator up to 0.95% of pool trades in the 420–1,470 SOL tier; no coin-creation fee; 0.015 SOL to open the PumpSwap pool — [Phemex comparison, updated 14 Oct 2026](https://phemex.com/blogs/pump-fun-vs-bonk-fun-memecoin-launchpads); same 1.25% / 0.30% figures attributed to pump.fun docs — [soltokencreator fees (per search snippet)](https://www.soltokencreator.io/blog/pump-fun-fees-explained)
- 9 Jan 2026: co-founder Alon said the creator-fee design "failed to produce durable trading incentives"; Dynamic Fees V1 "quickly attracted first-time creators" and shifted incentives toward "low-risk issuance rather than risk-taking trading"; issuance outpaced sustained secondary liquidity. New direction: "market-led" model where traders decide which tokens qualify for fee rewards; already shipped: creators can split fees across up to 10 wallets, transfer coin ownership, revoke update authority — [Coinspeaker via Yahoo Finance, 10 Jan 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/pump-fun-admits-creator-fees-160120983.html)
- Creator Fee Sharing mechanics (official docs): `sharing_config` PDA per mint; initial shareholders `[(creator, 10,000 bps)]`; `update_fee_shares_v2` enforces max 10 shareholders, no duplicates, each share > 0, sum = 10,000 bps, and sets `admin_revoked = true` making it effectively single-use (immutable after set); distribution is permissionless; SOL goes directly to shareholder wallets — [pump-public-docs CREATOR_FEE_SHARING.md](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/instructions/CREATOR_FEE_SHARING.md)
- Trader Cashback mode (creator chooses creator fees OR cashback to traders before launch) was introduced in response to criticism that few traders profit — [Cryptorank (per search snippet)](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/40a6d-pump-fun-launches-trader-cashbacks-in-fee-model-shift). - Other 2026 doc updates: fee sweeps (protocol and creator fees can be left on curve/pool and swept permissionlessly; fees kept in pool are subtracted from `virtual_quote_reserves`); synthetic migration (the buy that empties the curve can take tokens priced at the PumpSwap pool price); USDC quote mint; 30 Sep 2026 `Pool::virtual_quote_reserves` can be negative; 13 Oct 2026 depth-2 pump-coin quotes — [pump-public-docs README](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs)

### Inferences
- The industry converged on a ~1–1.5% headline take on the curve, with the design battleground being *who gets it*: pump.fun oscillated between platform (2024), 50/50 with creator (May 2025), heavy creator skew by market-cap tier (Sep 2025), and then back toward traders/holders (cashback → holder rewards, 2026) after concluding creator fees rewarded issuance rather than trading.
- Every platform that tied its token to fees (PUMP, BONK, BOOP, STONK, LAUNCHCOIN) used buyback-and-burn as the headline value accrual; pump.fun is the only one that made the commitment contractually irreversible (12 months from Apr 2026). Bonk.fun's replacement of burn with treasury purchases for a Nasdaq-listed entity coincided with (but is not proven to have caused) its >99% revenue decline.

### Gaps

## Key Question 2: What share of launched tokens graduate, and what happens to graduated tokens' liquidity?

### Takeaway
Graduation rates are low and definition-dependent: cohort-based measures for pump.fun run ~1.4–2.7% (Aug 2026 deployer stats: 1.8%), while same-day ratios reported by The Block fell as low as 0.26% in Jun 2026. ### Inferences
- Any new launchpad should expect ≤2% graduation on cohort basis and should design fee economics for the 98% that never graduate (that is where pump.fun's 0.95% protocol take on the curve is earned) rather than for post-graduation volume.

## Key Question 5: What anti-bot or fairness features exist, and did they work?

### Inferences
- No platform has published data showing its anti-sniping measure reduced bot share; the measures that demonstrably changed behavior were economic-routing rules (immutable fee recipients, holder rewards), not curve-shape or time-lock tricks. A new launchpad would likely get more trust mileage from immutable, holder-directed fee routing and bundle-visibility than from a 60-second cap.
