# Launchpad growth drivers and product utility (creators and traders), as of October 2026

Scope note: These notes cover user acquisition/retention drivers for token launchpads from a product-utility angle (feeds, terminals, mobile, social, creator tools, incentives, platform tokens, distribution, and the 2025-2026 "launchpad wars"). Smart-contract mechanics are out of scope. All dates are as stated by the source; where a source's publication metadata conflicts with the events it describes, that is flagged. Several sources are SEO/affiliate or exchange-blog pages; those are marked "(low-reliability)" and their numbers should be treated as indicative only.

Date-handling caveat for the whole file: many pages surfaced by search carry 2026 publication stamps but describe events that other, better-dated sources place in 2025 (e.g. the LetsBonk July surge). I note each instance. The freshest hard-data snapshots used are OpenChainBench (measured 2026-10-19, Allium-backed tehcscreener API), a Bitquery investigation (window 3 Aug-3 Sep 2026), a Pine Analytics/TechFlow report on Raydium LaunchLab (published 2026-10-19), and a crypto.news Q4 2026 memecoin outlook (2026-10-18).

## Key Question 1: What share of launchpad trading volume happens through third-party terminals and bots versus the launchpad's own UI, and what does that imply for where fees and utility should live?

### Takeaway
Most trading of launchpad tokens does not happen on the launchpad's own site: on the best available frontend-attribution data, pump.fun's own interface ("Terminal", the rebranded Padre) routed roughly 7-8% of daily Solana spot volume in June 2026 versus ~45% for Axiom, and on 2026-10-19 pump.fun's frontend routed ~$33M/day while trades on pump.fun-launched tokens totalled ~$446M/day across all venues (~7%). The protocol fee on the bonding curve/AMM is therefore the durable revenue layer; the interface layer is contestable, and pump.fun's response has been to buy terminals (Padre, Vyper) rather than rely on its own UI.

### Inferences
- Two independent measurements (Blockworks June 2026 frontend share; OpenChainBench Oct 2026 routed vs token volume) both put the launchpad's own interface at single-digit to low-double-digit share of trading on its own tokens. The implication for a new launchpad: protocol-level fees (curve + AMM take) capture value regardless of UI, while UI-level fees only capture what the launchpad's own frontend routes. A launchpad should expect to earn on the pool, not the page.
- Because terminals aggregate every launchpad, a new launchpad's tokens must be indexed by Axiom/GMGN/Fomo/Phantom from minute one to be tradable at all; "integration with terminals" is a go-to-market requirement, not an optional feature. The Heaven case (concern about a "liquidity island" if not integrated with Jupiter routing) illustrates the risk.
- pump.fun's acquisition path (Kolscan wallet tracker, Padre terminal, Vyper, perps) shows the incumbent concluded the same: own the execution layer or be disintermediated. Its Terminal still holds <10% share after a year of ownership, suggesting terminal loyalty is sticky and hard to buy.
- The spread between Fomo's volume ($52.9M) and its fees ($270K, ~0.5%) vs Axiom ($66.2M, $590K, ~0.9%) suggests fee competition at the terminal layer is real but not decisive; Axiom retains the lead at a higher take rate.

### Gaps
- No source provides a clean split of pump.fun-token volume by originating frontend (e.g. "X% of pump.fun bonding-curve volume came via Axiom"). The Blockworks table is Solana-wide spot volume; OpenChainBench explicitly refuses to combine the two benches.
- No reliable 2026 figure separating "bots" (automated/sniper) from "terminals" (human-operated UIs); the 60-80% bot figure is 2025-era and from a secondary report.
- Blockworks' "Making sense of launchpad wars data" article returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.

## Key Question 2: Which features correlated with measurable user growth or share gains (with dates and numbers)?

### Takeaway
Across the 2025-2026 launchpad wars, every challenger that took meaningful share did so with an incentive change (higher creator fee split, revenue buyback/burn, holder rewards, zero gas), and every one of those surges reverted within 4-8 weeks: Believe (May 2025, ~$14.7M revenue month, 0.4% share by August), LetsBonk (78% share late July 2025, 3% by late August), Heaven (28% first week Aug 2025, <1% by September), Bags (42.6% volume share mid-Jan 2026, single digits weeks later), Pons (beat pump.fun on daily fees from Aug 29, 2026, revenue down ~97% from peak by early October), StonkFun (95% of LaunchLab launches in Sept 2026, launches down 39% in early October). The only durable product-feature effects with numbers are pump.fun's Dynamic Fees ($2.1-2.4M paid to 5,640 creators in 24h on Sept 2-3, 2025) and the Base app's auto-minting of content coins (1.6M tokens, ~3M traders, $470M volume in ~3 weeks of July-Aug 2025), both of which also faded.

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern is "incentive shock, 2-8 week share spike, reversion." Share is extremely mobile (LetsBonk went 0 to 78% to 3% within ~6 weeks; Bags 0 to 42.6% to single digits within ~3 weeks) because traders follow terminals, not launchpad UIs, and terminals list every pad; switching cost for a trader is zero. Durable share has accrued only to pump.fun, which has the deepest terminal integrations, the PumpSwap AMM, owned execution (Terminal), and the largest buyback.
- Features that measurably created new users (as opposed to shifting existing traders) were distribution features: Base app auto-minting (3M traders), Phantom in-wallet trading (18.8% of frontend volume), and Pons on Robinhood Chain (35K first-time wallets in one day). Fee-split changes shifted creators and volume but did not create sustained new demand.
- StonkFun and Pons both found demand in "non-SOL quote assets" (tokenized stocks, ETH, stablecoins); pump.fun's copycat Custom Pairs did not take, suggesting first-mover plus a novel reward mechanic (holder taxes paid in the quote asset) mattered more than the incumbent's distribution.
- Mobile has been weak for launchpads specifically (pump.fun app ~50K downloads, <2.5 stars) while wallet-native trading (Phantom) is strong; a new launchpad likely gains more from being inside wallets and terminals than from shipping its own app.

### Gaps
- No 2026 download/MAU data for the pump.fun app, Moonshot, or Bags mobile; no Phantom-attributed user counts for any launchpad.
- No source quantifies the effect of feed design ("king of the hill," trending, new-pairs) on retention; all feed-related material found was descriptive.
- Livestreaming: no viewer counts, number of streams, or revenue attributable to streams were found.
- Referral/points programs: terms found only for terminals (Trojan, Axiom, Terminal), not for launchpads; no data on referral-driven acquisition share.
- Launchpad aggregators (e.g. Jupiter's launchpad data, Axiom's multi-pad "pulse"): no usage figures found; the only aggregator-related evidence is that terminals effectively serve as the aggregator layer.

## Key Question 3: What do creators say they need (fee visibility, payouts, marketing tools, vesting), and which platforms serve that best?

### Takeaway
Direct creator testimony is thin in the sources; what exists points to three things creators respond to: a high, visible fee split paid continuously (Bags 1% with up to 90% routable to any X/TikTok/Kick/GitHub account; Pons ~70-80% of ~1-1.5% fees; pump.fun up to 0.95% on small caps), fee routing to a verified social identity without the recipient's involvement (the Bags/Gas Town mechanic that produced ~$300K for one developer), and fast CTO/fee-claim processing. Vesting, anti-sniper and LP-lock tools exist (Jupiter Studio, Raydium LaunchLab Fee Key NFT, Zora's 5-year creator vest) but no source links them to measurable creator adoption.

### Cited Findings
- Bags: 1% fee on all volume; creators keep at least 10% by default; fees can route to a recipient identified by Twitter/TikTok/Kick/GitHub account who can receive up to 90% and "need not know about the launch"; anyone can launch a token for someone else's project; fee sharing with "up to 100 creators, apps or wallets," API-level fee customization, social verification and community-funding features. — [Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/bags-launchpad); [Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/analysis/best-memecoin-launchpads)
- Gas Town outcome: Yegge initially saw ~$49K then $75K+ in fees, later said he had received nearly $300K, and "stepped back from the community." — [Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/bags-launchpad)
- Pons creator economics: $9.7M of $11.8M monthly fees paid to 103,904 creators (~80%); median $15.73; the 70/30 split "is cited as the reason creators keep launching tokens." — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pons-launchpad); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pons-vs-pumpfun-launchpad-war-robinhood-chain/)
- Pump.fun Ascend rationale: "running a project is expensive and flat fees don't work, while raising fees across the board would hurt traders"; creators could use fees "for marketing, content, or exchange listings"; CTO creator-fee applications processed 10x faster. — [Blocmates](https://www.blocmates.com/news-posts/pump-fun-s-project-ascend-promises-10x-creator-earnings-but-traders-ask-what-about-us)
- Jupiter Studio: configurable LP fees, optional LP locking, vesting and anti-sniper tools. Raydium LaunchLab: creators earn fees before and after migration via a Fee Key NFT; referral commissions on eligible trades. — [Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/analysis/best-memecoin-launchpads)
