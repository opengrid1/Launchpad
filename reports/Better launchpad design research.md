# Rebuild OneHook on Proven Launch Plumbing

The evidence-backed launchpad design is deliberately boring: an immutable, swap-only Uniswap v4 hook that charges a bounded fee in ETH at the pool level, the full supply seeded into concentrated single-sided positions held by a locker with no withdraw path, one pre-committed decaying launch fee as the anti-snipe layer, and fee recipients fixed at launch. That pattern is shared by the three hook launchpads with real traction: Clanker (**$92.5M lifetime fees**, almost all on Base), Doppler (**6M+ pools, 93% of v4 pools on Base**) and Flaunch, and it is the configuration that survived two years of exploits, scanner flags and user backlash ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/summary/fees/clanker?dataType=dailyFees); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/387485/token-creation-platform-doppler-seed-round-pantera-capital)). OneHook v2 already has the skeleton (factory-held permanent liquidity, ETH-denominated hook fee, decaying buy tax) but layers on mechanics the evidence grades as gimmicks or liabilities: ETH dividends to holders (every volume-funded reflection token on record collapsed with volume), reflexive buyback-and-burn (no measurable price effect even at protocol scale, and scheduled slippage-unprotected burns are a standing sandwich target), a max-wallet cap (defeated by bundlers that openly sell 16-17-wallet launches), and milestone "evolution" that has no evidence base beyond marketing-grade tokens. Nothing at the contract level has been shown to retain traders: **79% of pump.fun launches trade only on launch day, graduation runs at or below 2%**, and the only variables empirically associated with survival are social presence and the creator's own self-buy ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/memecoin-outlook-for-q4-2026-can-the-sector-find-lasting-buyers/); [arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3)). Retention comes from distribution, not tokenomics: launchpads' own interfaces route roughly **7% of the volume on their own tokens**, and every incentive-driven share grab of 2025-26 (LetsBonk 78% to 3%, Bags 42.6% to single digits, Pons down ~97% from peak) reverted within weeks ([OpenChainBench](https://openchainbench.com/benchmarks/trading-platform-wars); [Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/bags-launchpad)). On the chain question the data is unambiguous: no Ethereum mainnet launchpad has ever sustained volume (Clanker's mainnet hook has earned **$61.7K** against $92.2M on Base; Ethervista and Unicurve trade tens of dollars a day), and while gas is cheap today (0.159 gwei, a swap ≈ $0.14) it can rise 100x within days and the public mempool makes sandwiching endemic. The recommendation is therefore two-tiered: strip OneHook to the proven core (fee-only hook, locked multicurve liquidity, immutable ETH fee split skewed to creators, single decaying launch fee, audit and bounty before any marketing) and ship that same contract set where distribution exists, on Base or on Robinhood Chain where the owner already operates Safehood, while repositioning the mainnet deployment as a low-frequency venue for larger, scheduled ETH- or stablecoin-quoted launches rather than a pump.fun clone.

## Distribution, not mechanics, decided every launchpad war

The single most important finding across the notes is that traders do not use launchpads; they use terminals and wallets that list every launchpad. Blockworks frontend attribution for June 4, 2026 put Axiom at **44.6% of Solana spot volume**, Phantom at 18.8%, and pump.fun's own Terminal at **7.7%**, and OpenChainBench's October 19, 2026 snapshot shows the same shape: pump.fun's frontend routed ~$33M/day while trades on pump.fun-launched tokens totalled ~$446M/day across all venues ([Moby, citing Blockworks](https://moby.win/best/solana-trading-apps-2026/); [OpenChainBench](https://openchainbench.com/benchmarks/trading-platform-wars); [OpenChainBench launchpad wars](https://openchainbench.com/benchmarks/launchpad-wars)). The incumbent reached the same conclusion and bought the execution layer (Kolscan, Padre, Vyper), yet its Terminal still holds under 10% a year later ([Cointelegraph via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:38b828f1a094b:0-pump-fun-moves-deeper-into-trading-infrastructure-with-vyper-acquisition/)). The implication for OneHook is structural: the pool-level fee is the only revenue a launchpad can rely on, and being indexed by terminals, wallets and aggregators from the first block is a go-to-market requirement rather than a feature.

The second finding is that incentive changes buy share for weeks, never months. Believe peaked at ~$14.7M monthly revenue in May 2025 and was at 0.4% share by August; LetsBonk reached **78% share in late July 2025 and 3% by late August**; Heaven routed 100% of revenue into buybacks, took 28% share in its first week and was under 1% by September; Bags hit 42.6% of daily launchpad volume in mid-January 2026 on the "Gas Town" fee-routing story and fell to single digits within weeks; Pons beat pump.fun on daily fees from August 29, 2026 and had lost roughly 97% of peak revenue by early October ([Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/bags-launchpad); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pons-vs-pumpfun-launchpad-war-robinhood-chain/)). The two features that measurably created new users rather than shifting existing ones were distribution features: the Base App's auto-minting of a coin per post (**1.6M tokens, ~3M traders, $470M volume in about three weeks**) and Pons on Robinhood Chain (**35,008 first-time wallets in a single day** during the chain's gas waiver) ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/zoras-creator-coins-push-base-past-solana-in-daily-token-launches-but-will-it-last/); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pons-launchpad)). Both faded once the distribution partner stepped back: Zora's daily volume fell **99.8% from its peak** and Jesse Pollak conceded the creator-coin bet failed ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/jesse-pollak-base-zora-social-bet/)).

Platform tokens and buybacks show the same non-effect on usage. LetsBonk cut its BONK burn from 50% to 40% to 35% and removed it on December 3, 2025 as share collapsed; pump.fun burned ~$370M of PUMP on April 29, 2026 and moved from 100% to 50% revenue buybacks explicitly to fund product and acquisitions, while monthly revenue had fallen 77% year on year; Heaven's "god flywheel" failed fastest of all ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/blogs/pump-fun-vs-bonk-fun-memecoin-launchpads); [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/pump-fun-burns-370-million-pump-tokens-revenue-buyback/); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/heaven-memecoin-launchpad-buys-back-everything)). Across five platforms with buyback programs, none shows a retention effect in launches or trader counts; the programs track token price, which is a token-marketing outcome, not a product one.

Against this backdrop, the base rate for any launched token is death. Solidus Labs found **98.6% of pump.fun tokens** with at least five trades fell below $1,000 liquidity; a 2026 cohort of 832,941 launches graduated at a lower-bound **0.198%**; median hold time for Solana memecoins fell to ~100 seconds; Virtuals' Robinhood Chain launches graduated at 0.36% with 64.5% never trading at all ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance); [arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3); [Cointelegraph](https://regional-front.cointelegraph.com/news/memecoins-attract-users-platforms-reap-profits-report); [ChainWard](https://chainward.ai/decodes/virtuals-top-dog)). In the one rigorous study that looked for correlates, launches advertising a Telegram graduated at 1.485% versus 0.166% without, and all three social links versus none was a **17.4x** difference; no contract-level mechanic appears anywhere in the literature as a predictor ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3)). Pump.fun's own co-founder said in January 2026 that creator fees "failed to produce durable trading incentives" and rewarded "low-risk issuance rather than risk-taking trading" ([Yahoo/Coinspeaker](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/pump-fun-admits-creator-fees-160120983.html)). A new launchpad should therefore design its economics for the 98% of tokens that never graduate and its product for the terminals that will actually route the volume.

## The proven contract pattern: fee-only hook over permanently locked multicurve liquidity

Four v4-hook launchpads have been audited, deployed at scale and criticised in public, and they converge on the same contract shape. Clanker v4 is a non-upgradeable factory plus a hook whose only jobs are setting the LP fee per swap (static or volatility-based, **capped at 30%**) and skimming a protocol fee equal to **20% of the LP fee, taken only in the paired token** via beforeSwap/afterSwap deltas; the full supply goes into up to seven single-sided positions minted to a locker that has "no code path" to transfer the NFT and "no liquidity-removal function," and the anti-snipe is a pluggable MEV module that runs for at most two minutes (two-block delay, sniper auction, or a parabolic fee descending from up to 80%) ([Clanker docs](https://clanker.gitbook.io/clanker-documentation/references/core-contracts/v4); [ClankerHook.sol](https://github.com/clanker-devco/v4-contracts/blob/main/src/hooks/ClankerHook.sol); [ClankerLpLockerFeeConversion.sol](https://github.com/clanker-devco/v4-contracts/blob/main/src/lp-lockers/ClankerLpLockerFeeConversion.sol)). Doppler wraps the same idea in modules behind an Airlock orchestrator: a multicurve initializer places a staircase of up to ~100 concentrated positions, a fee-decay hook drops the fee linearly (Bankr's config: **80% to 1.2% over 10 seconds**), a `NoOpMigrator` that always reverts makes liquidity permanent, and the protocol takes a **5% minimum** of fees ([Pastor Sanchez](https://www.ignaciopastorsanchez.com/blog/bankr-s-migration-to-doppler-protocol-a-technical-deep-dive); [Doppler.md](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler/blob/main/docs/Doppler.md)). Zora's hook uses only `afterInitialize` and `afterSwap`, seeds Doppler multicurve positions, applies a **99% sniper tax decaying over 10 seconds**, and re-mints 20% of fees as out-of-range single-sided liquidity so fees become permanent depth ([Zora docs](https://docs.zora.co/coins); [Zora support](https://support.zora.co/en/articles/2509953)). Flaunch runs a fixed-price fair-launch window, captures a 1% fee in ETH, splits it by an immutable creator percentage, and streams the remainder into a resting bid one tick below spot ([Flaunch docs](https://docs.flaunch.gg/features/auto-buybacks.md); [Flaunch docs](https://docs.flaunch.gg/features/creator-revenue.md)).

| Design element | Clanker v4 | Doppler (Bankr/Zora config) | Flaunch | Evidence verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Where the fee lives | Hook, paired token only | Hook, LP fee + beneficiaries | Hook, ETH via flETH | Pool-level fee is table stakes; token-level taxes break 1inch (49% cap), Trust Wallet (blocks unknown tax) ([1inch](https://help.1inch.com/en/articles/5651059-what-is-a-fee-on-transfer-token); [Trust Wallet](https://support.trustwallet.com/support/solutions/articles/67000740000--swap-is-blocked-by-asset-tax-value-error-what-it-means)) |
| Liquidity custody | Locker, no withdraw path | Initializer, `NoOpMigrator` reverts | Hook-managed | "No withdraw function" is the checkable claim scanners and traders reward |
| Initial curve | Up to 7 positions | Up to ~100 positions | Single tick then pool | Multicurve cuts modelled sniper-extractable value **77.5%** ([holosko](https://paragraph.com/@holosko/i-rebuilt-dopplers-auction-math-from-the-whitepapers-what-the-curves-actually-do)) |
| Anti-snipe | ≤2 min MEV module, ≤80% fee | 80%→1.2% over 10 s; 99%→0 over 10 s | 30-min fixed price, CAPTCHA | Making early blocks expensive beats restricting them |
| Protocol take | 20% of LP fee | 5% minimum | 0-10% by governance | Bankr left Clanker for Doppler citing fees ([Pastor Sanchez](https://www.ignaciopastorsanchez.com/blog/bankr-s-migration-to-doppler-protocol-a-technical-deep-dive)) |
| Creator share | Up to 7 recipients, mutable by admin | 95% in Bankr's config | 0-100%, immutable | Immutable or one-shot recipients survived; mutable ones were "vamped" |
| Fee recycling | None | Rehype: 20/20/30/30 buckets | Progressive bid wall | Only maker-side resting bids have a defensible MEV story |

Three lessons from this family matter directly for OneHook. First, the residual admin surface that scanners and critics flag on Clanker is the mutable reward recipient with no timelock, which "cannot touch liquidity but can redirect future fees"; pump.fun had to cap fee-recipient edits to one per token in March 2026 after "vamping" drained trust ([ClankerLpLockerFeeConversion.sol](https://github.com/clanker-devco/v4-contracts/blob/main/src/lp-lockers/ClankerLpLockerFeeConversion.sol); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pump-fun-locks-creator-fees-after-vamping-drains-trust-on-solana-industry-reaction-snowballs/)). Second, creators migrate toward higher visible splits paid continuously in the quote asset: Pons paid ~80% of fees to 103,904 creators, Bags lets up to 90% route to a social handle, Doppler's Bankr config pays creators 95%, and the Bags mechanic of routing fees to an unclaimed verified identity produced the only documented viral creator event (~$300K to one open-source developer in days) ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pons-launchpad); [Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/bags-launchpad)). Third, headline fees that hide a stack ("0.7%" that is really 1.75% at Bankr, "1%" that is 1.2% at Clanker) are a recurring criticism, so the all-in rate should be the number on the page ([Bankr docs](https://docs.bankr.bot/token-launching/overview/); [Clanker docs](https://clanker.gitbook.io/documentation/general/creator-rewards-and-fees)).

Revenue from this pattern is real but violently cyclical. Clanker's fees were roughly **$35M in Q1 2026 and a few thousand dollars per day by mid-October 2026**; Flaunch's cumulative fees are $3.6M with daily fees between $0.29 and $12.52 in the week of October 13-18, 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/clanker); [DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/summary/fees/flaunch?dataType=dailyFees)). Even the best-designed hook launchpad is a cyclical business tied to a distribution channel, which is the right frame for deciding how much to spend building one.

## Scoring OneHook's instincts against the evidence

OneHook's docs describe a fee in ETH on every swap (1% default, 0.10%-3% bounds), a buyback pot and a dividend pot each capped at 50% of the fee and 80% combined, a 60/40 creator/platform split of the remainder, a buy-only anti-snipe tax starting at up to 30% and decaying linearly over an opening block window, a max-wallet cap during an opening window, an "MEV priority-fee tax," and "evolution": up to five pre-committed market-cap milestones that can only lower fees, raise reward shares and ease friction. The v2 README records that the first mainnet deployment kept liquidity inside the hook and was flagged by scanners as a critical "liquidity drain," which drove the move to factory-held positions with no collect path. That move was correct and is exactly the lesson the rest of the industry learned. The remaining instincts score unevenly.

| OneHook mechanic | Keep, change or drop | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fee charged in ETH at the hook, bounded 0.1-3% | Keep | Matches Clanker/Flaunch; avoids every token-tax penalty in aggregators and wallets |
| Full supply single-sided, factory-held, no withdraw | Keep, extend to multicurve | Already the Clanker locker property; a staircase of positions is the only anti-snipe lever with a quantified model |
| Decaying anti-snipe buy tax (≤30%) | Keep, raise the ceiling, shorten the window, publish parameters | Industry settled on 80-99% start decaying in 10 s to 2 min (Doppler, Zora, Clanker); 30% is too low to deter a block-0 bundle. Meteora exposes its schedule in docs so terminals can price it ([Meteora docs](https://docs.meteora.ag/meteoras-anti-sniper-suite-a.s.s./meteoras-anti-sniper-suite/fee-scheduler)) |
| 60% creator / 40% platform | Change to 80/20 or lower platform take | Creators chase visible splits; 40% is at the Epochpad end of the market, which has no volume ([Epochpad](https://epochpad.fun/)) |
| Dividend pot (ETH to holders pro rata) | Drop | Volume-funded reflections die with volume: RFI "crashed to virtually zero," SafeMoon filed Chapter 7, EverETH shows ~$181 of daily volume against a $647K cap ([bitcoinethereumnews](https://bitcoinethereumnews.com/finance/did-reflection-tokens-peak-or-are-they-just-starting-out/); [KoinX](https://www.koinx.com/blog/is-safemoon-dead); [FXEmpire](https://www.fxempire.com/crypto/evereth/profile)). StonkFun's holder rewards: 82% of 289K recipients sold or moved them ([airdropalert, low reliability](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/what-is-stonkfun/)) |
| Buyback-and-burn pot | Replace with a resting bid wall or Zora-style LP contribution, or drop | Keyrock found no consistent price effect across 550 buyback days and showed programmatic buybacks overspend at peaks; scheduled burns with `amountOutMinimum = 0` are a reported sandwich target ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/designing-token-buybacks/); [sqrtDAO issue #119](https://github.com/sqrtDAO/contracts/issues/119)) |
| Max-wallet cap in opening window | Drop | Defeated by wallet-splitting; bundlers advertise "add up to 16 more" wallets; deployers fund their own same-block snipers with 87% profitable snipes ([Smithii](https://smithii.io/en/pump-fun-bundler-bot/); [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)); GoPlus flags `anti_whale` controls ([GoPlus](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/response-details)) |
| Evolution (milestone stages) | Collapse into one monotonic fee schedule | Every "evolving tokenomics" example found is marketing-grade; the only defensible form is a pre-committed, decrease-only fee by age or FDV, which one hook on Unichain implements as a single schedule ([hooklist #3516](https://github.com/Uniswap/hooklist/pull/3516)). Five stages multiply audit surface for no measured benefit |
| MEV priority-fee tax | Keep only if measured cheap and simulable | No outcome evidence in the notes; Trail of Bits warns against fees derived from cheaply manipulable inputs and searcher heuristics exclude pools whose `beforeSwap` simulates above ~200k gas ([Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/07/30/building-secure-uniswap-v4-hooks/); [FRB Research, vendor](https://dev.to/frb_research/uniswap-v4-hooks-mev-2026-searcher-opportunities-and-risks-1kkk)) |
| "AI" framing | Drop the label | OneHook's own docs say the system is "deterministic, not an AI model"; Epochpad and Hooky market AI-steered hooks on mainnet with no measurable volume ([Hooky](https://hookypad.fun/)) |

Two of these deserve elaboration because they are the product's headline. The dividend pot has a second cost beyond dying with volume: OneHook's `OneDivToken` variant tracks per-holder accounting in the token's transfer path, which is precisely the kind of non-standard transfer behaviour that scanners probe and that makes a token a second-class citizen in routers. Scanners penalise modifiability and privilege rather than the fee level, but Trust Wallet's hard block on any token whose tax "cannot be determined" means anything the simulator cannot resolve is treated worse than a known 5% tax ([GoPlus](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/response-details); [Trust Wallet](https://support.trustwallet.com/support/solutions/articles/67000740000--swap-is-blocked-by-asset-tax-value-error-what-it-means)). A plain, ownerless, non-taxed ERC-20 with all logic in the hook is the configuration that renders green today, because no scanner found analyses hook logic at all. The buyback pot's best replacement is not a burn but liquidity: Flaunch's Progressive Bid Wall converts fees into a 0.1 ETH limit order "immediately below spot" that follows price up, and Zora's "market contribution" re-mints 20% of fees as single-sided out-of-range liquidity so fees become permanent pool depth ([Flaunch docs](https://docs.flaunch.gg/features/auto-buybacks.md); [Zora support](https://support.zora.co/en/articles/2509953)). Both turn fee revenue into something a trader or a liquidity check can see, which burns and reflections never do. Neither has published fill or survival data, so this is a design argument rather than a measured result, and the honest position is that fee destination matters far less than whether a token attracts volume at all.

The anti-snipe conclusion is firmer. Per-wallet caps and CAPTCHAs are restrictions, and restrictions are evaded by splitting; a decaying fee or Dutch-auction start price is paid by every wallet regardless of how many there are. Doppler's whitepaper claims instantaneous arbitrage cost deployers over $100M on mainnet; an independent rebuild of its math shows multicurve cutting modelled sniper-extractable value from 80,000 to 18,020 under identical demand, while noting the results are model-only and the whitepaper's Zora claim does not reproduce ([holosko](https://paragraph.com/@holosko/i-rebuilt-dopplers-auction-math-from-the-whitepapers-what-the-curves-actually-do)). The one empirical sniper-cohort paper withdrew all its numbers in October 2026 after discovering 43% of its "buy" rows were sells ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02795)). So the state of the art is: structure the opening with a steep, short, published fee decay over a multicurve, accept that outcome data does not exist, and generate it yourself.

## The trust floor: simple hooks, published audits, immutable recipients, geofencing

Both large v4 hook losses came from hooks that did more than fee logic. Cork lost ~$11-12M on May 28, 2025 because `beforeSwap()` could be called directly rather than only by the PoolManager, and three of its four audits did not cover the hook; Bunni lost ~$8.4M on September 1-2, 2025 to a rounding bug in its custom liquidity-distribution math that v4 flash accounting netted as a positive balance ([Cork post-mortem](https://www.cork.tech/blog/post-mortem); [Rekt](https://alpha.rekt.news/bunni-rekt)). Trail of Bits' July 2026 checklist is the most concrete public guidance: restrict `beforeInitialize` so random pools cannot attach to your hook address, gate every callback to `msg.sender == PoolManager`, keep permission bits in sync with implemented return deltas, prefer immutable versioned deployments, bound every fee, never let non-essential logic revert in `afterRemoveLiquidity`, and fuzz the invariants that no output goes uncharged and no same-transaction round trip creates value ([Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/07/30/building-secure-uniswap-v4-hooks/)). BlockSec adds that the PoolManager checks only conservation, not correctness, so a hook returning a fabricated delta still settles ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/lead-in-uniswap-v4-hook-risks)). OneHook takes its fee through beforeSwap/afterSwap return deltas exactly as Clanker does, which is a legitimate and audited pattern, but it is the pattern that must be audited: OneHook's security page says an external audit "is planned," while the product is live on mainnet with user-launched coins. Clanker published four audits across versions (Quantstamp, Macro, Cantina), Doppler had OpenZeppelin and Certora reviews plus a Cantina contest and live bounty, and Uniswap v4 itself set the ceiling with nine audits and a $15.5M bounty ([Clanker audits](https://www.clanker.world/docs/references/audits); [Doppler security](https://docs.doppler.lol/resources/security-and-bug-bounties); [Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswap-v4-is-here)). Stripping the dividend accounting, buyback execution, max-wallet and five-stage evolution out of the hook is also the cheapest way to shrink the audit: the complexity budget is the audit budget.

Trust is communicated by what is checkable on-chain and by precise wording. Clanker's locker "lacks a method to withdraw it and cannot be upgraded," a claim anyone can verify; GoPlus only shows LP as locked if the locker contract is on its list, so a new launchpad must get its locker whitelisted with GoPlus, De.Fi, TokenSniffer and Honeypot.is or its LP shows as "held by an unknown contract" ([Paragraph](https://paragraph.xyz/@matthewb/clanker-pt1); [GoPlus](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/response-details)). Uniswap's interface runs Blockaid warnings for honeypots, impersonators and 100% sell fees and frames them as "not investment advice," which is the right template for risk labels ([Uniswap support](https://support.uniswap.org/hc/en-us/articles/8723118437133)). Because GoPlus reports `pool_fee` for v4 pools, a very high launch-phase fee can read as an alarming number, so the launch window should be short, documented, and exposed through a `getCurrentFee()` view. The single highest-value disclosure is creator and promoter allocation: the pump.fun order criticised 25 unnamed KOLs for "concealing both their compensation and their own preexisting positions," and the Believe class action alleges the founder collected ~$54M in creator fees on tokens he claimed "zero ownership" of ([Protos](https://protos.com/solana-labs-dropped-from-burwick-laws-pump-fun-lawsuit/); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/class-action-claims-believe-founder-collected-54m-while-diluting-token-holders/)). Show the reserved first buy, the fee split and the recipient addresses on the token page forever, and make recipients immutable or one-shot editable.

The regulatory picture is manageable for a non-custodial design and hostile to anything that looks like gambling or revenue sharing. The SEC staff statement of February 27, 2025 says most memecoins are not securities because there is no common enterprise and no expectation of profit from others' efforts, while warning that labels "used to evade" securities law are disregarded and fraud remains actionable ([SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/staff-statement-meme-coins)). On August 31, 2026 Judge McMahon dismissed the Securities Act claims against pump.fun for lack of a common enterprise but let RICO claims survive on predicate acts of wire fraud, illegal gambling and unlicensed money transmission ([Protos](https://protos.com/solana-labs-dropped-from-burwick-laws-pump-fun-lawsuit/)). The CLARITY Act failed cloture 49-50 on September 15, 2026, so there is no federal safe harbour ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/news/clarity-act-cloture-vote-fails-49-50-september-2026)). A launchpad that holds no user funds, has no operator withdraw authority and charges transparent fees directly addresses the money-transmission and gambling theories; one that pays ETH "dividends" to holders out of trading fees undercuts the collectible framing that makes memecoins non-securities, which is a further reason, beyond the economics, to drop the dividend pot (this is an inference from the SEC's own reasoning, not a holding). In the EU the MiCA transition ended July 1, 2026 and the Commission's February 2026 answer says issuerless tokens need no white paper but platforms still need CASP authorisation and must assess whether an issuer exists; a launchpad whose creators are identifiable and receive fee streams is a weak candidate for the carve-out ([Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/ar/news/mica-transitional-period-ends-1-july-2026/); [ESMA Q&A 2552](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2552)). The UK FCA warning that led pump.fun to geoblock the UK in December 2024 is the clearest launchpad-specific enforcement signal ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/fca-issues-warning-bans-pump-fun-uk)). No launchpad found requires KYC; geofencing the UK and EU on the front end while leaving the contracts permissionless is the de facto standard.

## Mainnet is unserved because it is under-demanded, which sets the architecture

Every source agrees that nothing measurable launches on Ethereum mainnet. Clanker's mainnet hook has been live since October 2025 and earned **$61,698** all-time against **$92,221,047** on Base; Ethervista, launched in 2024 as a "pump.fun rival on Ethereum," trades ~$54-77K a day almost entirely in its own VISTA token; Unicurve.fun showed ~$55 of daily volume; Epochpad, Hooky and BunkerPad exist with no volume data; Coin Bureau's July 2026 guide names no mainnet launchpad at all ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/summary/fees/clanker?dataType=dailyFees); [Bybit](https://www.bybit.com/en/price/ethervista/); [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/unicurve); [Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/analysis/best-memecoin-launchpads)). Uniswap Labs itself put its launchpad, pools.trade, on Robinhood Chain, where it did ~$73.6M of v4 volume on day one versus ~$47.2M on all of Ethereum mainnet the same day ([CoinReporter](https://www.coinreporter.io/2026/08/uniswap-labs-launches-memecoin-launchpad-on-robinhood-chain/); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/robinhood-chain-uniswap-v4-pools-trade-launch)). Nobody tracks mainnet launchpads because nothing is there, and no source documents latent demand.

The gas argument has weakened but not vanished. Etherscan's October 19, 2026 snapshot shows **0.159 gwei and a swap at $0.136**, which makes a ~250k-gas hook swap roughly $0.10 and a ~2M-gas launch under $1; at 15 gwei those become ~$9 and ~$72, and at 50 gwei ~$30 and ~$240 ([Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/gastracker)). Base swaps cost $0.01-0.05 ([Cryptsy](https://cryptsy.com/crypto-transfer-fees/)). Mainnet remains 100-1000x Base per transaction and spikes 100x within days, which specifically punishes Clanker-style per-swap auto-claims and fee-conversion swaps and OneHook's per-swap pot accounting. MEV is worse: a 2026 study finds sandwiching "endemic and profitable on Ethereum L1" and "rare, unprofitable, and largely absent in rollups with private mempools," and EigenPhi data put L1 sandwich losses near $60M a year ([arXiv 2601.19570](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19570v1); [Cointelegraph Research](https://cointelegraph.com/research/exclusive-data-from-eigenphi-reveals-that-sandwich-attacks-on-ethereum-have-waned)). Any mainnet front end must default to Flashbots Protect or MEV Blocker. On the positive side, v4 now carries more than half of Uniswap's mainnet volume, hooks reached double-digit shares in 2026, and 1inch, CoW, ParaSwap and Matcha index hook pools their solvers can simulate, so a cheap, bounded, fee-only hook is routable; a pending Uniswap governance move to switch on v4 protocol fees (10 bps default, up to 250 bps for "aggregator hooks") could add to the stack and should be monitored ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/en-uniswap-v4-captures-arc-and-robinhood-chain-volume-as-hooks-drive-liquidity); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/fr/crypto/uniswap-v4-explained); [Uniswap gov forum](https://gov.uniswap.org/t/temp-check-activate-v4-protocol-fees/26162)).

The niches with demonstrated 2026 demand all live elsewhere and all involve the quote asset rather than the token. Pons had over a third of 208K coins backed by tokenized stocks, commodities or wBTC and describes itself as the only launchpad letting creators choose ETH, a stablecoin or tokenized assets as backing; StonkFun's stock-paired coins were 31.5% of Solana's tokenized-equity DEX volume and 95% of Raydium LaunchLab's September launches; pump.fun's copy, Custom Pairs, "failed to capture attention" ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pons-launchpad); [Pine Analytics via KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/raydium-q3-fee-surges-3x-q4-growth-uncertain-amid-stonkfun-dependency); [AMBCrypto via Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605820390)). Robinhood Chain's zero-gas window from July 1 to roughly September 29, 2026 coincided with the Pons surge and its expiry with the collapse ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pons-vs-pumpfun-launchpad-war-robinhood-chain/)). The owner already has a live V3 launchpad, Safehood, on Robinhood Chain, which is the one chain where an ETH-denominated retail launch loop has demonstrably worked, if only with subsidised gas and Robinhood's brand.

### The recommended architecture

Put the contracts where the chains differ and keep one code base. The hook should be swap-only (beforeSwap, afterSwap, both return-delta flags), immutable, with `beforeInitialize` restricted so only factory-created pools attach, every callback gated to the PoolManager, a hard-coded fee ceiling, and no privileged fee path. The token should be a plain ownerless fixed-supply ERC-20 with no transfer-path logic, so it renders green on GoPlus and Blockaid and quotes cleanly in every router. Liquidity should be the full supply in a multicurve staircase of concentrated positions held by the factory with no decrease-liquidity function, which OneHook v2 already guarantees for a single position; ten to twenty positions capture most of the modelled anti-snipe benefit without Doppler's full hundred. The only launch defence should be a published fee schedule starting at 80-99% on buys and decaying linearly to the base fee over 10 seconds to 2 minutes, exposed via a view function and a chart on the token page, with sells never blocked. The fee should be charged in the quote asset at 1% all-in, split 80% creator / 20% platform by default, with recipients fixed at launch or editable exactly once, up to a handful of recipients so fees can route to a verified social identity, and a per-token page that shows the split, the recipient addresses and the creator's reserved first buy permanently. If fees are recycled into the token at all, route the community share into a resting bid one tick below spot or into out-of-range single-sided liquidity, never into scheduled market buys. Drop the dividend pot, the max-wallet cap, the five-stage evolution and the "AI" language; fold the "fee only ever falls" idea into a single monotonic schedule by pool age or FDV if a second stage is wanted at all. Allow ETH, USDC and, where available, tokenized-asset quote pairs because the quote asset is where the 2026 demand signal was. Before any marketing: one audit whose scope explicitly covers the hook, factory and router, a public report, an Immunefi bounty, a published fuzz suite, locker whitelisting with GoPlus and peers, listings on the Uniswap hooklist and on the indexers terminals use, geoblocking of the UK and EU on the front end, and a pre-published incident playbook with no pause authority over user exits.

| Decision | Recommended | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Chain for the retail product | Base (Farcaster, Bankr, Zora, Doppler integrators) or Robinhood Chain (owner's Safehood, Pons precedent, pools.trade) | Base has incumbents with distribution; Robinhood Chain's demand collapsed when the gas waiver ended. Mainnet retail is a thesis with no supporting data |
| Role of the mainnet deployment | Low-frequency venue for larger, scheduled ETH/USDC-quoted launches with private-RPC default and aggregator-routable hook | Earns little until a hit; competes with Doppler-style scheduled sales rather than pump.fun; must survive 50 gwei days |
| Fee level and split | 1% all-in, 80/20 creator/platform, protocol take visible | Lower platform take than today's 40%; creators choose venues on split, and the 98% dead-token long tail means volume per launch matters more than take rate |
| Fee recycling | None by default; optional resting bid wall or LP contribution | Visible depth, but adds hook complexity and audit cost; no published outcome data for either |
| Anti-snipe | Steep short published fee decay over multicurve | Deters block-0 bundles economically; does not stop deployer self-buys, which need funding-graph labelling on the front end ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)) |
| Creator alignment | Immutable/one-shot recipients; optional vest of the first buy locked atomically at launch | Zora's 5-year vest is the strictest design in production and still did not produce durable tokens ([Zora docs](https://docs.zora.co/coins)) |
| Holder rewards | Drop | Loses a marketing hook; gains scanner cleanliness, lower gas, and a cleaner non-security posture |
| Platform token / buybacks | None until the platform has organic volume | Forgoes a short-term price narrative; avoids the LetsBonk/Heaven/Believe failure mode and the unfulfilled-airdrop liability |

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem the owner is trying to solve. OneHook was built on the premise that cleverer tokenomics retain traders, but the measurable history of 2025-26 says retention is bought by whoever routes order flow (terminals, wallets, social feeds) and that the only durable asset a launchpad owns is a clean, audited, pool-level fee on tokens those routers are willing to index. The product that wins on that definition is almost the opposite of an "evolving AI" launchpad: a hook so simple that an auditor can cover it in one engagement, a token so plain that every scanner passes it, a liquidity position so obviously unremovable that the claim needs no trust, and a fee schedule so legible that solvers price it correctly. Each instinct OneHook removes lowers its audit bill, its per-swap gas, its scanner risk and its regulatory exposure at the same time, which is a rare alignment.

The chain decision follows from the same logic. Ethereum mainnet is not where retail launch loops happen, and no source offers evidence that it could be; the gas story has improved enough to make mainnet a credible home for a smaller number of larger, deliberately scheduled launches where ETH-denominated conviction capital and aggregator routing are advantages, but not for a pump.fun-style feed. The pragmatic path is one simplified contract set deployed twice: on mainnet as the "serious launch" venue, and on a chain with native distribution, where the owner's Safehood foothold on Robinhood Chain and the Base integrator ecosystem are the two live options. Whatever is left unmeasured in the notes (bid-wall fills, Dutch-auction outcomes, deployer-funding detection on EVM) is also the opening: a launchpad that publishes its own outcome data on these questions would be the first to do so, and in a sector where every challenger's share reverted within weeks, verifiable honesty is the one feature nobody has yet copied.
