zk shielded pool / ethereum

Private ETH transfers that don't pretend to be magic.

Umbra is a fixed-denomination shielded pool. You deposit one ETH against a secret, wait for other deposits to pile up, then withdraw to a brand new address. The blockchain stays fully public. The link between the address that paid in and the one that pulled out does not.

See how a deposit works Read the contracts 
// deposit: the chain only ever sees a hash
commitment = Poseidon(nullifier, secret)
pool.deposit{value: 1 ether}(commitment)

// withdraw: prove you own a leaf, never say which one
proof = prove(secret, merklePath)
pool.withdraw(proof, root, nullifierHash, newAddress)
// 1 ETH lands at newAddress. no trail back.

The part most privacy sites skip

Zero-knowledge proofs do not invent a crowd for you. Every deposit and every withdrawal is visible on Etherscan. What the proof hides is which deposit a withdrawal came from. So your privacy is exactly as large as the number of other people who deposited the same amount. No more.

1 in the pool with you
No cover. With one depositor, the withdrawal can only be you.

Practical rule: use a fresh recipient address, and do not withdraw seconds after you deposit. Timing links you even inside a large pool.

How a transfer goes through

  1. 01

    Deposit against a secret

    Your wallet picks two random numbers, a nullifier and a secret. It hashes them into a commitment and sends one ETH plus that commitment to the pool. The chain stores the commitment as a leaf in a Merkle tree. The two random numbers never leave your machine.

  2. 02

    Wait for company

    Nothing forces you to withdraw right away, and you should not. Every new deposit of the same size grows the set you can hide inside. Withdraw into a thin pool and the math protects nothing.

  3. 03

    Withdraw as a stranger

    From a fresh address, you produce a proof that you know the secret behind one of the leaves, without revealing which. The contract checks the proof, records the nullifier so the note cannot be spent twice, and releases the ETH. A relayer can post the transaction so the new address never needs gas.

Privacy without being a laundromat

A pool that takes anyone's coins becomes a magnet for stolen ones, and then it gets sanctioned and everyone honest loses access. Umbra ships with a screening hook at the deposit door. Point it at an allowlist, an attestation reader, or an association-set provider. Funds that fail screening never enter.

The hook is one interface. Set it to the zero address and the pool runs with no screening at all. That is a deliberate choice you make at deploy time, in the open, not a switch hidden in the code.

Under the hood

Proof system

Groth16 over BN254, one circuit, six public signals.

Hashing

Poseidon, in the circuit and on chain, so the roots agree.

Tree

Depth 20 incremental Merkle tree, last 30 roots kept.

Double spend

Each note reveals a nullifier hash, stored once, checked forever.

Front running

Recipient, relayer and fee are bound inside the proof.

Contracts

Solidity 0.8, Foundry tests run a real proof end to end.

Questions people actually ask

If I am the only one in the pool, am I private?

No. One deposit and one withdrawal of the same size, hours apart, is a straight line anyone can draw. The proof hides which leaf you spent, but there is only one leaf. Wait for others, or use a pool that already has depositors.

Can someone tell how much is in the pool?

Yes, exactly. The pool balance and the number of deposits are on chain for anyone to read. You cannot fake a crowd. That is the whole reason the size of the crowd matters.

Why fixed amounts instead of any value?

Amounts are a fingerprint. Deposit 3.7184 ETH and a matching withdrawal of 3.7184 ETH gives the link away even with a perfect proof. Uniform denominations remove that signal, so a thousand deposits actually act like a thousand.

Do I need ETH in my new address to withdraw?

No. A relayer can submit the withdrawal and take a small fee out of the amount. That avoids funding the fresh address from somewhere traceable, which would undo the privacy you just paid for.

Is this legal?

Privacy tooling is lawful in many places and restricted in others, and mixers on mainnet carry real regulatory weight. The screening hook exists for that reason. Talk to a lawyer who knows crypto before you run a pool that holds other people's funds.
