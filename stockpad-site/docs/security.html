Security

Security model

What can and cannot happen to a coin, its liquidity and its holders; the operator's powers; verification; and the risks of trading.

What cannot happen

  • The supply cannot grow: no contract has a mint function.
  • Liquidity cannot be withdrawn: the factory holds the launch position and has no function that removes it; the hook has no liquidity permissions.
  • Transfers cannot be blocked or taxed: the coin is a plain ERC-20 with no owner and no transfer logic.
  • A sell cannot be reverted by the hook, in any block.
  • A coin's fee, anti-snipe, holder-rewards share, pair and creator address cannot be changed after launch.
  • Fees owed to a creator can only be paid to the creator address recorded at launch; holder rewards can only be claimed by the holders they were recorded for.

The operator

The factory's admin is a fixed address set at deployment. Its powers are limited to the following:

  • pause new launches, and resume them;
  • hide a coin from the feed; the coin still trades;
  • set a coin's display metadata on the factory (the coin's own metadata is unchanged);
  • change the platform fee recipient;
  • block a token from being used as a pair in future launches;
  • change the opening market cap for future launches.

The operator cannot change an open market's fee, pair, liquidity or price, cannot pause trading, and cannot mint.

Every operator action emits an event (LaunchesPausedSet, HiddenSet, CoinMetadataSet, FeeRecipientSet, TokenBlocked, StartCapSet) and can be watched on-chain. The one-time setup rights of the deployer (wiring the router into the factory) were renounced after deployment; owner() on the factory returns the zero address.

Verification

The factory, the hook, the router, the token deployer and both coin variants are verified on Etherscan and on Sourcify with exact source matches. Each coin a user launches has the same creation code as the verified variant, so Etherscan shows it as a verified match without any action from the creator. Addresses and links are on the Contract addresses page. Review the verified source before relying on anything written here; the code is authoritative.

Risks

  • Price. A coin's price is set by trades in a pool seeded only with the coin itself. Early buyers set the price for later ones, and the price can return to the opening level if everyone sells.
  • Liquidity. All liquidity is single-sided and above the current price. Large sells move the price a lot; slippage protection on the site and in the router is your responsibility to set.
  • Stock tokens. A stock-paired coin's value tracks the stock token, whose price can drift from the real share while US markets are closed and which the issuer may restrict. Fees and rewards from such a coin arrive in the stock token, which US, UK and Canadian residents cannot hold according to the issuer.
  • Launch windows. Buying inside an anti-snipe window costs up to 30% extra, by design. Check the current buy fee on the coin page.
  • Routing. Trading a stock-paired coin in ETH passes through two more pools, each with its own fee and slippage.
  • Contracts. No audit is claimed on this site. The contracts are open source and verified; read them before relying on them.
  • Operator. The operator can hide a coin from the feed and pause new launches. Neither affects trading in an existing pool.

Disclosures

  • Equo does not custody funds. Coins are held in users' wallets, liquidity in the Uniswap v4 PoolManager, and unclaimed fees in the hook.
  • Equo does not promise or imply any return.
  • Coins launched through Equo are created by their creators. They are not securities issued by Equo.
  • Nothing on this site is financial, legal or tax advice.
