Overview

How it works

From the signed launch to trades, bridges, the last-buyer pot and the crash shield.

The launch

  1. Snapshot. The Equo launch service builds one snapshot for the whole launch and signs it (EIP-712): the name, ticker, metadata link, fee, the chain that pays the launch fee and the launch fee in USD, and one leg per chain with that chain's coin address, the salt that gives it, 1B coins and the USD price of the chain's native coin. Every EVM coin address ends in e9; the Solana leg names the coin's LayerZero OFT store and the creator's Solana wallet.
  2. The creator's chain. The creator's wallet sends the launch on its own chain with the launch fee and an optional first buy. The factory checks the signature and the legs, creates the coin, opens its pool at $3,000 for the 1B, puts the whole 1B in one position from the opening price up, pays the launch fee to the launch service and makes the first buy, fee-free, for the creator.
  3. The other chains. The service then sends the same snapshot to every other chosen chain. Each factory does the same, without a launch fee. HyperEVM's legs go in its large blocks, which come once a minute, so the service always sends them.
  4. Solana. The Equo program on Solana checks the same signed snapshot, makes the coin (Token-2022, 9 decimals, no freeze authority), mints Solana's 1B into a Meteora DAMM v2 pool against SOL at $3,000 and hands the mint authority to the coin's LayerZero OFT store. The service then sets the store up and wires it to every EVM chain of the launch. This takes a few minutes and several transactions.
  5. Wiring. On every chain the coin is linked at birth to its copies on the other chains (LayerZero peers, pinned message libraries, two verifiers), and the link is sealed: nobody can add, change or remove a peer afterwards.

A snapshot is good for at most an hour, and each launch happens once per chain.

Trading

Every EVM chain's pool is a Uniswap v4 pool between the coin and the chain's wrapped native coin. The Equo router takes the native coin for a buy and pays it for a sell; any router or terminal that can swap Uniswap v4 pools can trade them too. The hook takes the fee on every swap. A sell cannot take the price below where the pool opened: the router stops there and hands back the rest.

On Solana the pool is a Meteora DAMM v2 pool between the coin and wrapped SOL; the coin page trades it with a Solana wallet, and any app that routes Meteora pools can trade it too.

Bridging

The coin is a LayerZero OFT on every chain; on Solana it runs on LayerZero's official OFT program. Sending it burns it on the source and mints it on the destination. A send from an EVM chain leaves a 0.2% bridge fee behind; a send from Solana has none. Each route uses LayerZero's own message libraries and two verifiers pinned at deployment: LayerZero Labs and Twinstake between EVM chains, LayerZero Labs and Horizen to and from Solana. A send usually lands within a minute or two.

The last-buyer pot

0.1% of every trade on a chain goes to that chain's pot. A buy of at least $50 (at the native price of the launch) makes its wallet the last buyer and starts a 20-minute timer; the next such buy takes the spot and restarts it. When the timer runs out, the first trade after it sends 80% of the pot to the last buyer's wallet and 20% starts the next round. If nobody trades, anyone can send the prize with one call.

The crash shield

The hook watches each block's opening price. When it falls 20% or more below the highest opening price of the last 10 minutes, the shield goes up: sells pay up to 5% more, all of it into the pot, and buys pay up to half the fee less. It fades to normal over 30 minutes.
