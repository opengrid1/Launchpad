How it works

From the launch transaction to a claimed fee: what the factory, the hook, the router and the coin contract do.

LaunchOne transaction to the factory
Coin and pool1,000,000,000 coins seed a Uniswap v4 pool
Every swapThe hook takes the fee inside the pool
Fees paid outHolders first if on, then 60% creator, 40% platform

Equo is four contracts of its own plus Uniswap v4 and a price oracle. The factory launches coins and holds their liquidity, the hook charges the fee on every swap, the router lets the site trade any coin in ETH, and the token deployer creates the coin contracts. Nothing in the set can mint, pause, tax or move anyone's coins.

The contracts

ContractWhat it does
FactoryLaunches a coin in one transaction: deploys it, creates its pool, seeds the whole supply as a single position and keeps that position. Records the creator, the pair and the pool id. Has no function that removes liquidity.
HookAttached to every pool. On each swap it computes the fee (flat fee, anti-snipe during the launch window), takes it in the paired asset, and credits the creator, the platform and the holder-rewards pot. Anyone can trigger the payouts.
RouterTrades any coin in plain ETH. For a stock-paired coin it converts ETH to the stock along a route (WETH to USDC, USDC to the stock) before the pool swap, and back again on a sell. It holds no funds between calls.
Token deployerCreates the coin contract with CREATE2 at an address below the pair token's, so the coin is always currency0 of its pool, and hands the whole supply to the factory.
CoinA plain OpenZeppelin ERC-20 with EIP-2612 permits. No owner, no mint, no pause, no tax. Coins with holder rewards use a variant that adds pro-rata reward bookkeeping and a claim function.
Price oraclePrices the paired asset in USD for the opening price: Chainlink feeds for ETH and the stocks, pool TWAPs for other tokens. It is only used at launch and for display.
Uniswap v4 PoolManagerUniswap's singleton that holds every pool and executes swaps. Not a Equo contract.

The launch transaction

A call to launch on the factory does the following, in order.

  1. Price the pair

    The factory asks the oracle for the USD price of the paired asset (WETH when ETH is chosen) and reads its opening market cap setting, currently $5,000. From the two it computes the opening price of one coin in the paired asset: (cap / 1,000,000,000) / pair price.

  2. Deploy the coin

    The token deployer creates the coin with CREATE2, searching salts until it finds an address below the pair token's. The whole supply of 1,000,000,000 coins is minted once, in the constructor, and transferred to the factory. If holder rewards are on, the rewards-enabled variant is deployed instead.

  3. Initialise the pool

    A Uniswap v4 pool is created with the coin as currency0, the pair as currency1, a pool LP fee of 0, tick spacing 10 and the Equo hook attached, at the opening price.

  4. Register with the hook

    The hook records the coin, the pair, the creator address, the launch block and the fee settings. The hook re-checks the settings against its own caps, so a fee above 3% or an anti-snipe above 30% can never be registered.

  5. Seed the liquidity

    The factory places the whole supply (less a rounding reserve of 1012 wei, one millionth of a coin) into one single-sided position from just above the opening price up to the highest tick. The position is recorded in the factory and held by it. There is no withdraw function on any contract, so the position is permanent; coins leave it only when someone buys.

  6. Dev buy (optional)

    If ETH was sent with the launch, the router converts it to the paired asset and the factory swaps it in the fresh pool. The coins go to the creator. This buy pays the normal flat fee, not the anti-snipe fee, and it is the only buy the pool accepts in the launch block.

The opening price

Every coin opens at the same market cap in USD, whatever the pair. With the current setting of $5,000 and a supply of 1,000,000,000, the opening price is $0.000005 per coin. In pool terms that is converted into the paired asset: at a Tesla price of $400, one coin opens at 0.0000000125 TSLAon; at an ETH price of $4,000, at 0.00000000125 ETH. The pool price is rounded down to the nearest tick, so the exact opening price is a little below the target.

The opening market cap is a factory setting the operator can change between $1,000 and $100,000 for coins launched from then on. It never affects a coin that has already launched.

Every trade after that

  1. A swap reaches the pool

    From the Equo site, from Uniswap, from an aggregator or from a contract: the hook does not care who sends the swap. Buying means paying the paired asset for coins; selling means paying coins for the paired asset.

  2. The hook computes the fee

    The flat fee the creator chose, plus the anti-snipe surcharge if the coin is inside its launch window and the swap is a buy. The fee is always measured in the paired asset.

  3. The fee is taken

    When the paired-asset amount is the one the trader specified (an exact-input buy or an exact-output sell) the hook takes the fee before the pool swap; otherwise it takes it from the paired-asset output after the swap. Either way the hook emits FeeTaken(token, fee, bps) and keeps the fee as a claim on the PoolManager.

  4. The fee is credited

    The holder-rewards share, if any, goes to the coin's rewards pot. The rest is credited 60% to the creator and 40% to the platform. Nothing is paid out yet; the amounts accrue per coin.

Two rules apply to every swap. A sell is never blocked by the hook, in any block. And in the launch block itself the pool accepts no buy except the creator's dev buy, so nobody can land a buy in the same block as the launch.

Trading in ETH

A stock-paired coin is priced in its stock, but the Equo site always trades in ETH. The router does the conversion: on a buy it wraps ETH, swaps WETH to USDC on the Uniswap v3 0.05% pool, swaps USDC to the stock on the stock's own Uniswap pool, then buys the coin in its Equo pool; a sell walks the same path backwards and sends ETH. Buyers therefore never need to hold the stock token. Wallets that do hold the stock can trade with it directly through buyWithPair and sellForPair, or swap in the pool through any Uniswap v4 interface.

What never changes

  • The supply: 1,000,000,000 coins, minted once, with no mint function.
  • The pool: one Uniswap v4 pool per coin, with the hook attached and the liquidity position held by the factory.
  • The fee, the anti-snipe settings and the holder-rewards share: recorded in the hook at launch, with no function to edit them.
  • The creator address that receives the creator share.
  • The coin's own metadata (name, symbol, description, links, logo), which is stored in the token contract.
